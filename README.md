Browser prototype of the {4,5} hyperbolic workspace layout. No build step or server: run `xdg-open index.html` from the repo root.

Live demo: https://deedlefake.github.io/gnome-shell-extension-hyperbolic-workspaces/

Every tile is a workspace. The colored ones have windows and form one connected group; the rest are empty and look like the bare grid.

Controls:
- Windows take clicks in the focused tile and in every tile touching it, by an edge or just a corner; those tiles' outlines are a little brighter. Clicking a window there enters its workspace with that window focused and on top. Clicking anywhere else in the focused tile enters it, empty or not, keeping whichever window was focused. Clicking any other spot on a tile focuses that tile; the view centers it.
- Entering zooms the workspace up into a desktop. Its windows glide from their overview slots to their real places as it zooms, and back when it zooms out. Drag a title bar to move a window, click a window to raise it, and click its × to close it. "+ Window" opens one; "Overview" or Escape zooms back out onto the same workspace, focused. While you are in a workspace it stays put even with no windows left. Once you are back in the overview, an emptied workspace closes and a workspace that got its first window joins the group, both by the usual rules.
- Drag a window in one of those tiles to move it to any tile in view. The tile under the pointer gets a dashed yellow outline. The window keeps its place and lands on top. A workspace that loses its last window and an empty tile that gains its first go through the same rules as closing and opening: cut-off pieces slide back, a far tile slides over to the group, and the center follows.
- Drag to pan, from empty space on any tile, from a window out of reach, or from outside the disk. Right-drag or the wheel rotates.
- Hold the move key and drag a workspace to move it. The move key is Shift by default; the side panel switches it to Alt, Ctrl or Ctrl+Alt. Meta is left out because the browser and desktop take it. While you hover, the plane previews the result. Drop it on any empty tile; if that tile doesn't touch the group, it lands there and then slides back to the nearest tile that does. Drop it on another workspace to insert it there: that workspace, and any in a straight line behind it, move one tile on in the direction you came from, up to the first empty tile.
- Arrows move to a neighbor tile. PgUp/PgDn walk the workspaces with windows. Home or "Back to center" returns to the center tile, upright. A small ring marks it.
- When the group gets too small to make out (its largest workspace below about four rings' size, where windows stop being drawn), a yellow arrow fades in outside the disk's edge, pointing the way to the center tile. Clicking it goes back to the center. It fades out again by the time the group is three rings' size.
- O opens a window on the focused tile, C closes one, X closes all of them, Enter enters it. The first window on a tile away from the group slides that workspace over to the group, and the view follows it.

Stay connected: whenever closing a workspace's last window or moving a workspace splits the group, the biggest piece stays put and each smaller piece slides over, keeping its shape, until it touches the group again. A piece only slides, never turns: it moves along a straight line through one of its workspaces, and the rest of the piece comes along beside it. Nothing else moves.

Each workspace is a flat screen with its own down edge, one of its tile's four edges; its windows sit square to it. A workspace's first window picks the edge drawn nearest the bottom of the screen at that moment, so it starts upright. After that, whatever moves the workspace carries its down edge along: a sliding piece and a pushed insert chain move by a tiling symmetry, and the down edge rides that same symmetry onto the new tile, so windows never spin against their tile. A dropped workspace lands on the edge nearest to how it was drawn while dragged (a turn of at most 45 degrees). A window moved to another workspace takes that workspace's down edge. There is no shared down across the plane; this geometry can't have one. "Upright after move" and Home end with the focused workspace's down edge at the bottom of the screen; an empty tile uses its own frame.

The overview spreads each workspace's windows out like GNOME Shell's window picker (`UnalignedLayoutStrategy` in GNOME 51's `js/ui/workspace.js`): shrunk to one shared scale capped at 95%, upright in the workspace's flat screen, in rows that never overlap, with a gap between windows. Rows fill in order of window height on the desktop and each row runs left to right in desktop order. Opening, closing or moving a window makes the rest glide to their new slots over 200 ms. Clicking and dragging use the same slots. The desktop keeps windows at their real places.

The center follows the group. After any open, close or drop, if the center tile has no windows but some tile does, the center moves to the nearest tile with windows by edge steps; ties go to the oldest workspace. With no windows anywhere it stays put. Home, first-window placement and the split tie-break all use the current center.

The side panel switches the arrow key behavior, orientation, pan snapping and the move key. The defaults are "Always visual direction" for the arrow keys and "Upright after move" for orientation.
`node sim.js [ops] [seed]` runs random open/close/drag/move-window churn and checks that the group stays connected and that nothing moves except the insert chain, a newly opened far workspace, and cut-off pieces, each as the image of its own tiles under one tiling symmetry that is a slide (a hyperbolic translation, never a turn about a point), with a one-tile slide taken whenever one reconnects a two-piece split, and that every moved workspace's down edge is the image of its old one under that motion, so no window spins. `node far.js` checks that tiles stay exact far from the center. `node backtrack.js` prints the earlier backtracking numbers. `results.txt` has the output.
